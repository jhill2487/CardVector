# Putnam Principles

# Mission

Build the systems that enable Putnam Collectibles to become a scalable,
profitable, and enjoyable trading card business.

Every application, workflow, automation, and business process should contribute
to one or more of the following:

- Increase cash flow
- Increase listing velocity
- Reduce manual work
- Improve operational decision making
- Increase long-term profitability
- Build systems that scale beyond 10,000 listings

The software exists to serve the business.

The business does not exist to justify the software.

---

# Decision Filter

Before implementing any feature ask:

1. Does this improve cash flow?
2. Does it remove a real operational bottleneck?
3. Does it improve listing velocity?
4. Will it still matter at 10,000+ listings?
5. Is it simpler than the alternative?

If most answers are "No", the feature should normally be deferred.

---

These principles define the philosophy behind the Putnam ecosystem.

Unlike project documentation, these principles are intended to remain stable
over many years and guide every major decision involving Putnam Collectibles,
CardVector, and future projects.

---

# Governance

The Putnam Principles are the highest governing document in the repository.

All engineering standards, platform standards, documentation, and software
architecture should align with these principles.

Hierarchy:

1. Putnam Principles
2. CardVector Platform Vision
3. CardVector Platform Standards
4. AGENTS.md
5. PROJECT_STATUS.md
6. CHANGELOG.md

If guidance conflicts, the higher-level document takes precedence.

---

# Principle 1 - Cash Flow First

The primary purpose of Putnam software is to help Putnam Collectibles generate
cash flow.

Software development should never significantly reduce the speed of:

- acquiring inventory
- listing inventory
- selling inventory
- shipping orders

The business never waits for the software.

The software waits for the business.

---

# Principle 2 - The Business Is the Customer

Putnam Collectibles is the primary customer of every Putnam application.

Software should solve real operational problems experienced while running the
business.

Real-world experience drives development.

---

# Principle 3 - Production Before Perfection

A reliable workflow is more valuable than a perfect one.

Prefer systems that are:

- repeatable
- understandable
- dependable

over systems that are theoretically perfect but difficult to maintain.

---

# Principle 4 - Build Once, Benefit Forever

Automation is an investment.

Prioritize building systems that continue saving time over hundreds or
thousands of future work sessions.

---

# Principle 5 - Automate Repetitive Work

If a task:

- is repeated frequently,
- follows predictable steps,
- and does not require meaningful judgment,

it should eventually become automation.

Humans should spend time making decisions, not repeating processes.

---

# Principle 6 - Systems Before Scale

Every improvement should help the business grow from hundreds of listings to
tens of thousands without requiring proportional increases in manual work.

Build systems that scale.

---

# Principle 7 - Simplicity Wins

Complexity is expensive.

Prefer solutions that are:

- easier to understand,
- easier to maintain,
- easier to teach,
- easier to troubleshoot.

---

# Principle 8 - One Source of Truth

Information should exist in one authoritative location.

Avoid duplicate:

- pricing engines
- inventory records
- configuration
- business rules
- documentation

The repository, not chat history, is the source of truth.

---

# Principle 9 - Portability Matters

Projects should never depend on a specific computer or username.

Applications should resolve resources through centralized path management and
environment-aware configuration.

---

# Principle 10 - Measure Before Optimizing

Improve what can be measured.

When possible, base decisions on operational data rather than assumptions.

Track metrics such as:

- listing velocity
- inventory turnover
- average order value
- days to sale
- cash flow

---

# Principle 11 - Business Before Content

Content exists to document the business.

The business does not exist to create content.

Running a successful business always takes priority.

---

# Principle 12 - Authenticity Is an Advantage

The real workspace, real processes, and real challenges are part of the Putnam
brand.

Document the journey honestly.

Show improvement over time.

---

# Principle 13 - Equipment Must Earn Its Place

Every equipment purchase should remove a genuine bottleneck.

Avoid buying equipment because it is interesting.

Invest when it meaningfully improves:

- speed
- quality
- consistency
- profitability

---

# Principle 14 - Repeatable Workflows Become Standards

Whenever a process becomes routine, document it.

Successful workflows should become:

- checklists
- operating procedures
- automation
- software

---

# Principle 15 - Modular Systems Scale Better

Build independent modules with clear responsibilities.

Applications should work well together while remaining independently
maintainable.

---

# Principle 16 - Continuous Improvement

Every work session is an opportunity to improve both:

- the business
- the platform

Capture bottlenecks.

Solve them.

Repeat.

---

# Principle 17 - Constructive Pushback

Ideas should be evaluated on their impact.

Question proposals that reduce:

- scalability
- maintainability
- accuracy
- profitability
- listing velocity

The goal is better decisions, not automatic agreement.

---

# Principle 18 - Long-Term Thinking

Favor decisions that continue creating value years from now.

Optimize for sustainable growth rather than short-term convenience.

---

# Principle 19 - Stewardship

Treat the Putnam ecosystem as a long-term asset.

Code, documentation, workflows, and business knowledge should remain
understandable and valuable to future contributors, including your future self.

---

# Principle 20 - Leave It Better Than You Found It

Every meaningful change should improve at least one of the following:

- clarity
- reliability
- maintainability
- efficiency
- profitability

Continuous small improvements compound into extraordinary long-term results.

---

# Principle 21 - The Flywheel

Putnam Collectibles and the Putnam Platform improve each other continuously.

Running the business produces operational feedback.

Operational feedback improves the platform.

The improved platform makes the business more efficient.

The more efficiently the business operates, the more insight it generates.

This continuous improvement cycle is the long-term competitive advantage of the
Putnam ecosystem.
