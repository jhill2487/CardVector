# The Putnam Manifesto

Putnam Collectibles is not a software exercise. It is a real trading card
business, operated in the real world, with real inventory, real customers, real
cash flow, and real constraints.

The Putnam Platform exists because operating that business creates problems
worth solving. Cards must be identified, priced, listed, stored, shipped, and
measured. Repetitive work slows the business down. Unclear processes create
mistakes. Missing records hide useful lessons. The platform turns those
operational realities into systems that make the next work session better than
the last one.

The business comes first because the business is the reason the software has
meaning. Software that does not improve cash flow, listing velocity,
operational clarity, or long-term profitability is a distraction. The goal is
not to build elegant tools in isolation. The goal is to build dependable
systems that help Putnam Collectibles operate with more speed, confidence, and
scale.

The best software here should emerge from real operational experience. A
bottleneck discovered during listing is better evidence than a theoretical
feature idea. A repeated manual step is an invitation to automate. A confusing
workflow is a signal to simplify. The platform should listen to the business,
not interrupt it.

Documentation matters because the repository is the source of truth. Good
documentation preserves decisions, captures workflows, and lets future work
start from understanding instead of rediscovery. It keeps the system teachable.
It helps future contributors, including future versions of ourselves, know why
things work the way they do.

Systems matter because scale punishes improvisation. A few hundred listings can
survive manual habits. Ten thousand listings require repeatable workflows,
clear records, reliable paths, and tools that reduce effort instead of adding
friction. The point of the platform is to let the business grow without manual
work growing at the same rate.

Long-term thinking matters because every small improvement compounds. A cleaner
path resolver, a clearer work session record, a safer export process, or a
better decision filter may look small in isolation. Over time, those choices
become the operating advantage of the business.

Authenticity matters because the real journey is the brand. The basement
workspace, evolving systems, operational lessons, and practical constraints are
not flaws to hide. They are evidence that the platform is built from the truth
of the business it serves.

Continuous improvement is the flywheel. Running the business creates feedback.
Feedback improves the platform. The platform makes the business more
efficient. A more efficient business produces better information, sharper
decisions, and more opportunities to improve.

We are not building software.

We are building a better way to operate a trading card business.

The software is simply one expression of that mission.
